Why it's different

Recorders make you press record
It's always been recording

A rolling buffer holds the last five or ten minutes of your screen — nothing older is ever kept. Start it at login and forget it exists.

The bug shows up

The flaky test fails. The UI glitches once in a hundred runs. The thing your bug report can't describe — it just did itself, on camera.

You press one key

The buffer becomes an MP4 in your library. Trim it, cut the dead air, copy it into the bug report. Done before the adrenaline fades.

Nothing piles up

Expired segments are deleted as they age out, so an all-day session costs about 250 MB of disk instead of a hard drive.

Library

Every save, filed by day

Replays land in a library, not a downloads folder. Thumbnails, durations, grouped by day — find Tuesday's glitch on Friday without scrubbing filenames. They're plain MP4 files on disk, so everything works with everything.

Editor

Trim the boring parts, keep the evidence

Drag the handles, cut the middle, export. Cuts are lossless — Encore never re-encodes your video, so a ten-minute trim takes about a second and loses zero quality. MP4, GIF, or straight to the clipboard for your bug tracker.

Fully open source. Your screen never leaves your machine.

No account. No cloud. No telemetry. Replays live in a folder you choose, and deleting one deletes it. And you don't have to take our word for it — every line of code is public. That's the whole privacy policy.

Opening it the first time

Encore isn't notarized by Apple yet, so macOS will say it "cannot be opened because the developer cannot be verified". That's expected for an unsigned build, not a sign of anything wrong.

  1. Drag Encore into Applications.
  2. Right-click it (or Control-click) and choose Open, then Open again in the dialog. macOS remembers the choice.
  3. Grant Screen Recording when asked, then relaunch — macOS requires one restart after the first grant.
